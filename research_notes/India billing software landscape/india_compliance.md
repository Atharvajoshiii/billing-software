# India GST and movement compliance

**Research date / cutoff:** 19 October 2026. Rules and portal validations change by notification/advisory; the linked live official sources should be rechecked before implementation or release.

## Invoice, product tax data, and corrections

### Takeaway
A billing product needs an auditable GST document engine whose outputs depend on registration/taxpayer type, supply classification, customer/ship-to facts, place-of-supply rules, and document lifecycle. Tax rates and legal applicability are not a single static “GST %” field: they are notification-driven and classification-sensitive, so product defaults should be versioned, reviewable, and overridden only with an explicit reason.

### Cited Findings
- CGST Rule 46 calls for supplier name, address, GSTIN; a consecutive serial number unique for the financial year; issue date; recipient name/address/GSTIN or UIN where registered; specified recipient/delivery/state details for unregistered recipients on taxable supplies of at least ₹50,000; HSN or service accounting code; description; quantity/UQC for goods; total and taxable values after discount/abatement; tax rates and amounts by CGST/SGST/IGST/UTGST/cess; place of supply and State for inter-State supply; distinct delivery address where relevant; reverse-charge indicator; and supplier/authorized representative signature. [CBIC Rule 46](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html)
- The rules permit a single invoice-cum-bill-of-supply for a registered person supplying taxable and exempt supplies to an unregistered person; a bill of supply is used for exempt supplies and composition taxpayers. [CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html); [CBIC composition rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/composition-rules.html)
- For composition levy, the bill of supply must state “composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies”; composition taxpayers do not charge GST as a separately collected line item. Eligibility and conditions must be validated outside invoice formatting alone. [CBIC composition rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/composition-rules.html)
- CBIC publishes goods and services rates by tariff heading/service classification, including conditions and cess; classifications, exemptions, and conditions matter, so a rate lookup cannot safely rely on a small hard-coded set of slabs. [CBIC GST rates](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-goods-services-rates.html)
- HSN/SAC digit requirements are turnover- and document-context-sensitive. CBIC’s notification amendment says from FY 2021–22: taxpayers above ₹5 crore turnover in the preceding year report six digits; those up to ₹5 crore report four digits for B2B supplies. GST Portal guidance also flags six digits above ₹5 crore and allows shorter code entry in some cases, illustrating that UI validation should be based on current rule context rather than one universal length. [CBIC Notification 14/2022-CT](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/central-tax/NN-14-2022-English.pdf); [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR-1.htm)
- For goods, the IGST Act generally identifies place of supply by where movement terminates for delivery; bill-to/ship-to has a deemed place-of-supply rule. For services, place-of-supply rules vary by service category, with registered-recipient location as a general rule subject to exceptions. Supplier location versus place of supply determines inter-State versus intra-State status (and generally IGST versus CGST plus SGST/UTGST). [IGST Act, sections 7–12](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/IGST-bill-e.html)
- Rule 46 explicitly requires place of supply and State only for inter-State invoices; address of delivery is needed where different from place of supply. [CBIC Rule 46](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html)
- GST rates can include nil/exempt/non-GST, special rates and compensation cess; distinguish these states in the product because they do not necessarily have the same tax/ITC/reporting treatment. CBIC’s rate schedules identify commodity/service-specific treatment and cess, and its FAQs distinguish nil-rated/exempt/non-taxable categories. [CBIC GST rates](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-goods-services-rates.html); [CBIC GST FAQ](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/faq.html)
- GST legislation recognizes CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST, and cess; state/UT and place-of-supply choices determine which tax components apply. [CBIC “Know About GST”](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/about-gst.html); [IGST Act](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/IGST-bill-e.html)
- Credit/debit notes are statutory document types and must be reported in GST outward-supply statements. E-invoice data for notes to registered and unregistered recipients maps to distinct GSTR-1 tables. [CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html); [GSTN e-invoice/GSTR-1 advisory](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/E-invoice_Advisory_GSTR-1.pdf)
- For services, Rule 47 generally allows 30 days from supply to issue the tax invoice, with 45-day provisions for specified financial suppliers and exceptions for certain distinct-person supplies. [CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html)
- GSTN’s outward supply form guide lists invoice-level B2B, specified inter-State B2C, export/SEZ, credit/debit notes, state-level B2C summaries, advances/adjustments, amendments, nil/exempt/non-GST supplies, HSN/SAC summaries, and e-commerce operator supplies as GSTR-1 data categories. Threshold for B2CL was changed to invoice value above ₹1 lakh from August 2024 (previously ₹2.5 lakh). [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm); [GSTN GSTR-1 creation guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR-1.htm)

### Inferences
- Product data model should store a tax classification per item/service (HSN/SAC, effective-dated rate, exemption/nil/non-GST state, cess, tax-inclusive/exclusive policy), but calculate tax per invoice line from seller GST registration, buyer GSTIN/status, supply facts, place of supply, reverse-charge status, and date.
- Do not silently “fix” a filed invoice. Keep immutable issued-document snapshots, linked credit/debit notes and correction trails, and preserve the exact tax basis/rate source/date used for each document.
- Offer configuration for multiple GST registrations/GSTINs under one PAN, distinct invoice series per establishment, financial-year numbering, State/UT identity, ship-to/delivery address, export/SEZ, and bill-to/ship-to; add warning/review for ambiguous place-of-supply cases.
- The rates master should be centrally maintained and effective-dated from notifications, with human confirmation for product classification and a clear “tax advice not inferred from product name” boundary. Keep zero-rated, exempt, nil-rated, and non-GST cases distinct.
- A return-preparation/export layer should preserve line-level records but render the relevant GSTN table aggregates and audit reconciliation; do not treat GSTR-1 auto-population as proof of a correct/final filed return.

### Gaps
- This research does not resolve GST treatment for any particular SKU/service or mixed/composite supply; classification and rate require current tariff/notification analysis and may need professional review.
- Exact credit-note tax adjustment time limits and every category-specific invoice exception were not exhaustively verified here; implement from current consolidated Act/rules and notifications before launch.

## E-invoice and e-way bill integration

### Takeaway
For covered businesses, an invoice produced by the billing app must be registered with an authorized Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and enriched with IRN and signed QR data; transport workflows may also need EWB details. A web app should make these asynchronous government-system integrations resilient, but should not imply that every customer needs e-invoicing or that a generated PDF alone satisfies the mandate.

### Cited Findings
- The GSTN-authorized IRP mandate page lists the threshold progression and states the current e-invoicing threshold as aggregate annual turnover (AATO) ₹5 crore or more in any financial year from FY 2017–18 onward, effective 1 August 2023. It describes threshold at PAN-wide scale across GSTINs. [GSTN-authorized IRP mandate](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/einvoice-mandate/); [CBIC Notification 10/2023-Central Tax](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/central-tax/NN-10-2023-CT.pdf)
- The same mandate page says only regular taxpayers and SEZ developers can generate e-invoices (and details notified exclusions/exempt categories in the mandate material). Applicability must be checked against both turnover history and taxpayer/exempt-category status, not simply current-year sales. [GSTN-authorized IRP mandate](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/einvoice-mandate/)
- The IRP accepts invoice data in the prescribed INV-01 schema, validates it, assigns a unique IRN, digitally signs the invoice and returns a signed QR code; GSTN material describes a valid covered GST invoice as requiring a valid IRN. [GSTN-authorized IRP FAQ](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/faq-powered-by-irisirp/); [GSTN e-invoice glossary](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/news/pamphlet_e_invoicing_glossary_updated_17_08_2023_approved_final.pdf)
- E-invoice documents reported to IRP flow into GST system and are auto-populated into specified GSTR-1 tables, including B2B, reverse charge, export, and credit/debit note records. Taxpayers are advised to review; auto-populated data can be edited and the IRN provenance fields reset. [GSTN e-invoice/GSTR-1 advisory](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/E-invoice_Advisory_GSTR-1.pdf); [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm)
- Effective 1 April 2025, taxpayers with AATO ₹10 crore or more must report e-invoices within 30 days of invoice date; the IRP rejects older documents. The portal’s production release notes specify this applies to invoices, credit notes, and debit notes. [GSTN-authorized IRP advisory](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/revised-time-limit-for-e-invoice-reporting-for-businesses-with-aato-of-%E2%82%B910-crores-above/); [IRP release note](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/kb/viewing-e-invoices/)
- The official e-way-bill portal describes an e-way bill as generally required for movement of goods where consignment value exceeds ₹50,000, including movement for reasons other than supply and inward supply from an unregistered person; it notes exemptions and state-specific intrastate thresholds/requirements. [NIC E-Way Bill System](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/); [official EWB FAQ](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/ewb_qna.html); [official EWB FAQ on State thresholds](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq1.html)
- EWB form Part A includes recipient GSTIN, delivery place/PIN, invoice/challan number and date, goods value, HSN, reason, transport document number; Part B includes vehicle number. [NIC Form GST EWB-01](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/Documents/GST_EWB_01.pdf)
- Where e-way bill details (distance, transport mode, transporter ID and road vehicle details as applicable) are sent with invoice data to IRP, IRP can return the associated EWB along with IRN and signed QR. [GSTN-authorized IRP E-invoice/EWB guide](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/e-invoicing-and-e-way-bill-how-to-generate-ewb-along-with-irn/)
- EWB generation is dependent on GST Common Portal registration data; a submitted EWB cannot simply be edited—official FAQ says cancel and generate a new one for wrong entries. EWB validity normally depends on distance and transport mode; portal material reflects rule changes and exemptions. [Official EWB FAQ](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq1.html); [EWB rules on CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/ewaybill-rules.html)
- The NIC EWB developer portal’s July 30, 2026 release notes say proposed EWB closure and mandatory ship-to GSTIN changes were put on hold; the May 2026 update was withdrawn. This is a concrete reason to track live advisories and avoid baking announced-but-withdrawn schema changes as current obligations. [NIC EWB API release notes](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/apidocs/release-notes.html)
- The EWB portal lists mandatory two-factor authentication for taxpayers and transporters from 1 April 2025. [NIC EWB portal](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/)

### Inferences
- Design IRP/EWB integration as a separate compliance subsystem: idempotency keys, request/response retention, status tracking, retries with backoff, duplicate prevention, cancellation/replacement workflow, audit logs, and a clear distinction among “draft”, “issued locally”, “IRN registered”, and “EWB active”.
- Implement a routing decision at document creation based on PAN-level historical AATO, GST registration type, transaction/document type, and current exemption list; provide a way to record/refresh eligibility and an exemption reason.
- Generate PDF/print output only after IRP response where mandate applies, storing signed payload, IRN, acknowledgement, and QR representation. For offline/latency cases, show a legally cautious pending/error state rather than falsely displaying an unregistered invoice as compliant.
- EWB fields are supply/logistics facts distinct from invoice accounting: transport mode, transporter ID, vehicle/transporter document, distance, reason, delivery PIN and consignment-level value. Support EWB updates/cancellation and preserve links among invoice, dispatch, and EWB.
- Since EWB thresholds/exemptions can vary by state for intrastate movement and notified commodity exceptions, store configurable jurisdiction rules and link users to current portal/advisory rather than assume one national rule covers every movement.

### Gaps
- The EWB official FAQ itself warns that state/UT intrastate thresholds should be checked in the respective statutes/notifications; a complete state-by-state matrix was not collected for this assignment.
- The exact currently authorized IRP list/API access terms, endpoint uptime, rate limits, onboarding credentials, and offline contingency rules need a separate technical integration review.
- E-invoicing exceptions are notification-specific and can change; check current Notification 13/2020-Central Tax and amendments against the exact taxpayer profile before production.

## GST registration, returns, and jurisdiction operations

### Takeaway
The product must support the seller’s GST profile and filing cadence, rather than treat GST as a per-invoice toggle. Multi-state operations, composition scheme, regular registration, exports/SEZ, marketplace supplies, and return reconciliation create different obligations and should shape setup, permissions, and reports.

### Cited Findings
- GSTR-1 is a monthly/quarterly statement of outward supplies for normal and casual registered taxpayers; composition taxpayers, ISDs, TDS deductors, and TCS collectors are excluded from the standard GSTR-1 requirement described by the GST Portal. Nil GSTR-1 filing is still required for a period with no activity. [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm)
- The GST Portal guide says quarterly GSTR-1 is available to taxpayers meeting the stated turnover/option conditions (up to ₹5 crore); both GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are then filed quarterly, with Invoice Furnishing Facility available for relevant first-two-month B2B invoices. [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm); [GST Portal IFF FAQs](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/FAQs_IFF.htm)
- GSTR-1 includes outward invoice data, amendments, credit/debit notes, exports/SEZ, advances, HSN summaries, nil/exempt/non-GST supplies, and specified e-commerce supplies. [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm)
- The e-invoice process transfers registered e-invoice data into the GST system for GSTR-1 population, but the GSTN advisory still tells taxpayers to review the entries and allows manual completion where records are missing. [GSTN e-invoice/GSTR-1 advisory](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/E-invoice_Advisory_GSTR-1.pdf)
- Place-of-supply statutes distinguish intra-State from inter-State supplies and include State/UT and SEZ treatment; supplies to/by SEZ are treated as inter-State under IGST Act provisions. [IGST Act sections 7–8](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/IGST-bill-e.html)
- Composition taxpayers must issue bill of supply with the prescribed legend, and the composition option ceases if statutory conditions are no longer met. [CBIC composition rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/composition-rules.html)
- The GST Portal identifies QRMP eligibility as PAN-based aggregate annual turnover up to ₹5 crore in the current and preceding FY (where applicable) and requires filing the last due GSTR-3B; the portal’s current profile/return facilities control exact options. [GST Portal QRMP eligibility FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/FAQs_change_profile.htm)

### Inferences
- Onboarding should capture GSTIN/PAN, legal and trade names, registration type (regular/composition/casual/SEZ where relevant), registered State/UT, effective dates, invoice prefixes/series, financial-year turnover history, return frequency, and authorization for each GSTIN. One PAN may have several GSTINs whose aggregate turnover matters for e-invoice eligibility.
- Give users books-to-return reconciliation for GSTR-1 versus GSTR-3B and e-invoice feeds, plus exports in GSTN-compatible formats. Treat filing as a user-controlled, reviewable action with reports for mismatch, missing HSN, invalid state code, duplicate invoice, and out-of-period amendment.
- Design multi-location inventory and billing around GSTIN establishments as distinct registrations; inventory, fulfillment warehouse, seller GSTIN, buyer, and delivery location are separate dimensions.
- Exports, SEZ, e-commerce operator TCS/section 9(5), reverse-charge, advances, returns and amendments should be supported as explicit transaction dimensions rather than free-text notes, because they map to different reporting tables and tax logic.

### Gaps
- This is a compliance feature map, not a complete filing specification. Current return schemas, due dates, late fees, payment/ITC rules, annual returns, TDS/TCS, ISD, refunds, exports/LUT, and registration thresholds should be separately validated from live forms and current notifications before implementation.
- State-specific enrollment, professional tax, local trade licenses, retail metrology/Legal Metrology labeling, and sector-specific rules are outside this GST-only scope.
