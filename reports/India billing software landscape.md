# Build an India-ready billing platform

As of 19 October 2026, these six products are better understood as **three different business systems sharing an invoice screen**: retail tools optimize fast billing and stock; BUSY and TallyPrime connect vouchers, inventory, accounting and compliance; Zoho Invoice optimizes client invoicing and collections; Sleek Bill sits between document-heavy billing and small-business stock. A credible web product should first choose a primary customer and make one end-to-end workflow excellent, then expand into adjacent modules. Across all segments, the product foundation is company and tax setup, customer/vendor and item masters, linked sales and purchase documents, payments, inventory and reports. GST is not a single rate field: it depends on seller registration, item classification, customer and delivery facts, place of supply, transaction type and document date. This report compares the public feature surfaces, explains the shared operating model, and recommends a staged product plan; it does not claim access to competitors’ internal architecture or substitute for a production legal specification.

The practical segmentation is more useful than a feature-count contest. **Retailers and wholesalers** need quick keyboard/scanner billing, purchase receiving, stock availability and dues. **Accounting-led traders and manufacturers** need voucher discipline, ledgers, costing, approvals, GST reconciliation and potentially production. **Freelancers and service firms** need estimates, time/project or expense billing, recurring charges and customer payment experiences. BUSY/Tally, myBillBook/Vyapar, and Zoho Invoice anchor those jobs respectively; Sleek Bill overlaps the small-business document and stock use case. ## Treat billing as a connected transaction lifecycle

A robust product is a linked workflow, not a collection of forms. Setup establishes the legal entity, financial year, GST registrations, branches, invoice series, users and permissions. Item and party masters then supply defaults: units, prices, HSN/SAC, tax category, customer GST treatment, payment terms, credit limits and delivery addresses. Purchase orders and bills bring stock in; receipts, expenses and supplier payments update payables and cash/bank records. On the sales side, a quote or order can precede a delivery challan and invoice; the invoice records quantities, discounts, tax, due date and fulfillment location; a receipt or gateway payment reduces the balance. Returns, credit/debit notes, cancellations, stock adjustments and refunds need their own traceable links to the original transaction.

The shared functional modules are therefore **organization and user administration; customers/vendors; product/service catalog; sales documents; purchases and expenses; inventory and locations; payments and receivables/payables; GST configuration and compliance; reporting/export; and integrations**. Service businesses can replace stock movements with time sheets, projects and reimbursable expenses. Manufacturing and distribution add bill of materials, production, batch/serial traceability, landed cost or branch transfers. Do not assume every customer needs these advanced controls; the named vendors’ different scopes show why a target segment matters.

The strongest common design pattern is one transaction engine feeding multiple business views. Saving a purchase should affect supplier balance, cash/bank when paid, stock received and input-tax reporting; invoicing should affect sales, stock issued, customer balance and outward-supply summaries; returns and notes should reverse or adjust the appropriate linked effects. This is a **product architecture inference** from vendors’ documented cross-module workflows, not a claim about their internal database design. For integrity and auditability, preserve source documents and their revisions, then derive stock, balances and reports from traceable transactions rather than letting independent screens silently overwrite one another.

A counter flow should be optimized separately from back-office administration: find or scan item, enter quantity, apply allowed discount, choose customer and tender, save, then print or share. A cashier should be able to do that with a keyboard and common scanner/printer setup; a stock clerk needs receiving and transfers; an owner needs dues, cash and low-stock views; an accountant needs tax exceptions and reconciliation. myBillBook and Vyapar document scanners/barcodes/POS workflows, while Zoho Invoice’s portal/reminder/payment tools illustrate the service-client side. The exact offline, sync, printer and device behavior of competitors is not fully documented publicly. ([myBillBook PC billing](https://mybillbook.in/s/billing-software-for-pc/), [Vyapar POS](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-do-pos-billing-with-vyapar), [Zoho features](https://www.zoho.com/in/invoice/features/))

For reports, begin with operational answers rather than a large analytics menu: today’s sales and collections, customer outstanding and ageing, purchase/payable summary, stock on hand and stock movement, low-stock list, product/customer sales, cash/bank daybook, expenses and profit summaries. Then add GST sales/purchase summaries, HSN/SAC summary, return reconciliation and branch/godown views. myBillBook explicitly lists these report families and CA sharing; BUSY and Tally emphasize accounting and inventory reporting; Zoho allows filtered/exported sales reports. Report availability and exact definitions vary by product and plan. ([myBillBook business reports](https://mybillbook.in/s/features/business-reports/), [BUSY](https://busy.in/), [Tally inventory reports](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally/inventory-tally/), [Zoho sales reports](https://www.zoho.com/us/invoice/help/reports/sales.html))

## Build GST correctness into the document engine

A GST invoice needs more than a percentage. CBIC Rule 46 specifies supplier identity/GSTIN, a financial-year-unique consecutive serial and date, recipient details as applicable, HSN/SAC, description and quantity/UQC for goods, taxable value after discounts, applicable tax rates and amounts, place-of-supply/state for inter-State supplies, delivery details when relevant, reverse-charge indication and signature requirements. The exact field obligations depend on the transaction and recipient context. A product should render the right document type and fields from validated facts, rather than offer one generic invoice template with an editable “GST” checkbox. ([CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html))

Tax treatment needs effective-dated classification and rules. CBIC rate schedules include tariff/service categories, conditions and cess; nil-rated, exempt and non-GST supplies have different reporting/credit treatment. HSN/SAC length requirements depend on turnover and context; the cited CBIC amendment describes six digits above ₹5 crore preceding-year turnover and four digits up to ₹5 crore for B2B supplies, while GST Portal workflows also allow shorter code entry in some cases. Place of supply depends on the supply type, seller and recipient locations and delivery facts; goods movement and bill-to/ship-to have specific rules, and service categories have exceptions. These factors generally determine inter-State IGST versus intra-State CGST plus SGST/UTGST. ([CBIC rates](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-goods-services-rates.html), [CBIC HSN notification](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/central-tax/NN-14-2022-English.pdf), [GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR_1.htm), [IGST Act](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/IGST-bill-e.html))

Represent seller registration type and period, buyer GST status/GSTIN, ship-to and bill-to states, item tax category and HSN/SAC, supply purpose, reverse charge, export/SEZ status, discounts, cess and tax-inclusive/exclusive pricing as explicit inputs. Keep nil, exempt, non-GST, zero-rated and taxable cases distinct. Composition taxpayers issue a bill of supply with the prescribed composition wording and do not collect GST separately; exempt supplies may also need a bill of supply, and a combined invoice-cum-bill-of-supply is permitted for certain registered-person supplies to unregistered customers. A product’s tax master should be versioned and explainable, but SKU classification still needs human confirmation. ([CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html), [CBIC composition rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/composition-rules.html), [CBIC rates](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-goods-services-rates.html))

For reporting, GSTR-1 captures outward invoice details, exports/SEZ, notes, advances and adjustments, B2C summaries, HSN/SAC summaries, amendments, nil/exempt/non-GST supplies and specified e-commerce supplies. The GST Portal currently describes quarterly filing options for eligible taxpayers up to ₹5 crore and an Invoice Furnishing Facility for relevant B2B invoices in the first two months. E-invoice data can auto-populate specified GSTR-1 tables, but GSTN advises taxpayers to review the populated data; auto-population is not a proof of a correct final return. myBillBook says its GST reports do not file returns; Zoho Invoice documents direct GSTR-1 filing but excludes purchase-side GSTR-2 reporting; TallyPrime documents broader GST portal connectivity. Do not collapse “reports,” “export,” “portal upload,” “filing” and “reconciliation” into one feature label. ([GST Portal GSTR-1](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/GSTR_1.htm), [GSTN e-invoice advisory](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/E-invoice_Advisory_GSTR-1.pdf), [myBillBook GST reports](https://mybillbook.in/s/features/gst-reports/), [Zoho GST filing](https://www.zoho.com/in/invoice/help/gst/gst-filing.html), [Tally release features](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tallyprime-features-release-wise/))

As of the research cutoff, the GSTN-authorized IRP mandate page states e-invoicing applies to notified covered taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover of ₹5 crore or more in any financial year from FY 2017–18 onward, measured PAN-wide, subject to taxpayer-category exclusions. For AATO ₹10 crore or more, IRP reporting has been limited to 30 days from invoice/credit note/debit note date since 1 April 2025. The IRP validates prescribed invoice data, assigns IRN, signs the data and returns a signed QR code; covered invoices need valid IRN. Applicability must therefore consider historical aggregate turnover, registration type and exemptions, not just current company sales. ([IRP mandate](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/einvoice-mandate/), [CBIC Notification 10/2023](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/central-tax/NN-10-2023-CT.pdf), [IRP reporting time limit](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/revised-time-limit-for-e-invoice-reporting-for-businesses-with-aato-of-%E2%82%B910-crores-above/), [IRP FAQ](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/faq-powered-by-irisirp/))

E-way bills are a separate movement-of-goods workflow. The official portal describes a general requirement for movement where consignment value exceeds ₹50,000, including some non-sale movements, subject to exemptions and state-specific intrastate thresholds. EWB-01 includes invoice/challan, recipient and delivery place, value, HSN, movement reason and transport details; vehicle or transporter information can be supplied in Part B. Submitted records are not simply edited; official FAQ guidance directs users to cancel and generate again for incorrect entries. Portal changes can be proposed and withdrawn, so keep integrations versioned and advisory-aware; the NIC release notes record 2026 changes placed on hold. ([NIC E-Way Bill portal](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/), [EWB FAQ](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq1.html), [EWB-01 form](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/Documents/GST_EWB_01.pdf), [API release notes](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/apidocs/release-notes.html))

These rules are an **October 2026 research snapshot**, not a durable legal specification. Before shipping a compliance feature, verify current consolidated Acts/rules, notifications, schemas, exemptions and portal advisories for the seller profile and transaction. The notes do not settle every category-specific exception, state threshold, amendment deadline, payment/ITC obligation or exact authorized IRP/API integration term.

## Use an auditable web-first data model

A reasonable web product model would separate tenant/business, GST registration, branch or godown, user/role, party, item/service, effective-dated tax classification, document header and lines, payment, stock movement, accounting entry and compliance submission. Each document should record the specific seller GSTIN, series, financial year, tax inputs and calculation result used at issuance. The sales invoice should retain customer and delivery snapshots so later master edits cannot rewrite what was issued. Multiple GSTINs under a PAN should be represented as distinct operating registrations, while e-invoice eligibility can require PAN-level historical turnover and category checks.

This architecture is a **recommendation inferred from the workflows and compliance requirements**, not a description of BUSY, myBillBook, Vyapar, Zoho, Tally or Sleek Bill internals. Use immutable issued-document snapshots with linked cancellation, credit/debit note and amendment records; never silently mutate a filed/IRN-registered invoice. Maintain a stock movement ledger for receipts, dispatches, returns, transfers and adjustments, plus a payment/settlement ledger for receipts, refunds and allocations. Derived balances can be cached for speed, but retain the source transactions and audit trail needed to reproduce them.

Government integrations should be an independent boundary with request and response retention, idempotent submissions, retry/status handling, duplicate protection, cancellation/replacement paths and human-review queues. Distinguish draft, locally issued, IRN-pending, IRN-registered, rejected, cancelled and EWB-active states. Only show a compliance-ready invoice when the required external registration has succeeded. The actual IRP/GSP onboarding, authorized provider terms, limits, downtime policy and API schema need a separate technical verification; the public competitor material does not disclose their implementation.

A browser app also has physical-world dependencies. Confirm scanner, thermal printer, cash drawer, weighing scale and keyboard flows with target shops; plan for unreliable internet and make sync state visible if offline use is a requirement. Competitors advertise cross-device access, cloud sync or offline modes, but public pages do not establish their synchronization protocols, conflict rules, uptime or security/data-residency architecture. Do not infer a cloud stack from marketing language. ([myBillBook PC billing](https://mybillbook.in/s/billing-software-for-pc/), [Vyapar POS](https://vyaparapp.in/guides/how-to-do-pos-billing-with-vyapar), [Sleek Bill plans](https://sleekbill.in/pricing/))

## Sequence the product around one customer segment

A practical first release for a web-based Indian SMB product should target one archetype—e.g., a single-GSTIN retailer/wholesaler that needs fast sales, purchases, stock and GST-ready records. Start with business setup and roles, customer/vendor/item masters, tax configuration, sales invoice and receipt, purchase bill and payment, simple stock ledger, PDF/print/share, customer dues, sales/purchase/stock reports, backup/export and audit history. Include credit/debit notes, returns, cancellation and invoice numbering before calling the product production-ready for real billing; these are core lifecycle controls, not decorative later add-ons.

Next add the capabilities needed for daily operational scale: purchase orders and delivery challans, partial fulfillment and payment allocation, barcode lookup, stock adjustments and transfers, godowns, low-stock/reorder, batch/expiry or serial tracking where the chosen vertical needs it, role permissions, multi-device operation, reminders and integrations for payment links or messaging. The documented myBillBook and Vyapar flows are useful benchmarks for simple inventory-to-invoice behavior; BUSY/Tally indicate what becomes necessary for accounting-led or multi-location customers. ([myBillBook inventory](https://mybillbook.in/s/inventory-management-app/), [Vyapar order management](https://vyaparapp.in/free/inventory-management-software/order-management), [BUSY inventory](https://busy.in/accounting-software/inventory-management/), [Tally inventory](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally/inventory-tally/))

Then build compliance depth deliberately: configurable return summaries and GSTN-compatible exports, reconciliation against portal data, missing/invalid classification queues, e-invoice eligibility checks and IRP workflow, then e-way bills. Treat direct return filing and purchase-side reconciliation as distinct product investments requiring authorization, schema maintenance, customer review and operational support. Add multiple GST registrations, branch approvals, accounting ledgers, bank reconciliation, advanced valuation/manufacturing and multi-entity reporting only after interviews show that the target segment will pay for that depth. A service-business track could instead prioritize quotes, recurring invoices, project/time billing, customer portal, reminders and payment gateway support, using Zoho Invoice’s workflow as reference.

Monetization across these products commonly appears as plan/edition gates around users, devices/businesses, POS/barcode, inventory depth, reports, multi-location, manufacturing, compliance integrations and support; current prices are volatile and the public pages sometimes conflict. A product can test a simple core plan with paid upgrades for multi-user/branches, advanced inventory, compliance connectivity and accountant tools. Validate willingness to pay and support costs through pilots rather than copy a competitor’s published price. The clearer differentiation opportunities are hypotheses to test with SMBs: explain GST calculations and exceptions in plain language, deliver reliable spreadsheet/accounting migration, make returns reconcilable and reviewable, provide regional-language assisted setup, reduce counter friction, and support weak connectivity without hiding sync conflicts.

## Conclusion

The first strategic choice is not which competitor has the longest feature list; it is whether the product is **counter-first, accounting-first or service-invoice-first**. Build the common records and linked transaction lifecycle so the product can grow, but launch with one job and a clear scope boundary. In India, compliance reliability and correction history can become the product’s trust advantage, but only if tax behavior is versioned, explainable and maintained against live rules. Public sources reveal what competitors advertise and document; they do not reveal their internal architecture, true operational quality or every plan entitlement, so use this landscape to frame product discovery and validate the precise workflows with target users and a GST professional before implementation.
