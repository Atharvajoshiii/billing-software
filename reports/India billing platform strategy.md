# Build Ledgerwise Around One Daily Job

Ledgerwise can become a credible Indian billing and accounting SaaS, but it is not yet a full accounting system: today it is a single-business billing workspace with basic stock, purchases, payments, expenses, and preview reports. The strongest route is to start with one GST-registered retailer or wholesaler segment, make its daily sale-to-stock-to-books workflow dependable, and expand into broader GST operations and larger-business controls only after pilots confirm the need. The competitors named here are not interchangeable: myBillBook and Vyapar emphasize accessible shop billing and inventory, BUSY and TallyPrime go deeper into accounting and GST workflows, Zoho Invoice targets service invoicing while Zoho Books expands into accounting, and Sleek Bill spans small-business documents and stock. Ledgerwise’s next investment should therefore be the transaction, accounting, and compliance foundations under its current screens—not a long list of disconnected features. This research reflects public product and government materials checked on 17 November 2026; GST guidance and competitor plans must be rechecked before launch.

The practical market is a set of related jobs: fast counter sales, wholesale purchasing and credit, accountant-ready books, multi-location operations, and service billing. There is no reliable independent market-share or willingness-to-pay dataset in the reviewed sources that proves which named vendor is “most famous” or which new segment will switch. Vendor feature pages establish what they advertise, not actual product quality or retention. ([competitor research notes](../research_notes/India%20billing%20platform%20strategy/competitors.md), [India SME Forum survey](https://indiasmeforum.org/digishaastra/assets/docs/Final-META-Report-Card-2025.pdf))

A reasonable starting customer is a **single-location, GST-registered retailer/wholesaler with product stock, supplier purchases, customer credit, and monthly bookkeeping needs**. This is close to Ledgerwise’s current product primitives and lets the team test both counter work and business books. Choose one local vertical for the first pilot—such as hardware/electrical, mobile/electronics, garments, or general trade—because units, returns, pricing, stock attributes, and billing hardware differ. “Small shop through big business” is a long-term product direction, not a suitable first release promise. The India SME Forum’s 2024–25 survey of 7,835 MSMEs reports 46.2% fully offline and 52.6% having difficulty finding appropriate tools, but it is a broad digital-tools survey rather than a billing-software adoption estimate. ([India SME Forum, Meta Report Card 2025](https://indiasmeforum.org/digishaastra/assets/docs/Final-META-Report-Card-2025.pdf))

## Ledgerwise has a useful first slice, not books yet

The checked-in app implements email/password Supabase authentication, initial business setup, a single selected business workspace, product and contact records, sales invoices, purchase bills, stock adjustments, expenses, customer and supplier payments, a dashboard, printable invoices, CSV exports, and GST sales summaries grouped by the offered rates. Invoices and purchases call Supabase RPC functions that are expected to number documents and update stock; the README describes those backend behaviors. The UI supports partial payment recording and overdue/outstanding views. ([current platform audit](../research_notes/India%20billing%20platform%20strategy/current_platform_audit.md), [main app](../src/app/page.tsx), [README](../README.md))

The important boundary is that the repository does **not** implement a general ledger or statutory GST workflow. The GST report is marked “PREVIEW”; the README explicitly says returns, IRP registration, e-way bills, and legal validation are not connected. Product tax is a dropdown with five options and a single `gst_rate`, while invoices use product rates. The app has no visible chart of accounts, journal engine, trial balance, profit-and-loss, balance sheet, cash/bank ledger, bank reconciliation, input-tax-credit matching, return preparation, or registration-level filing workflow. Thus its dashboard totals should not be presented as complete accounts. ([README](../README.md), [GST reporting and product form](../src/app/page.tsx))

The project is currently a single large client-side page. It loads transactions into browser memory, with up to 1,000 rows for several data sets, computes dashboard/report figures client-side, and has no checked-in database migrations, schema, RPC definitions, service layer, or automated test suite in the visible repository. The README says the deployed Supabase project has row-level security, but the repository does not include policies or backend function bodies for audit. Before a compliance or accounting launch, make the database and rules repeatable and reviewable in source control; test tenant isolation, transactional posting, numbering concurrency, and recovery. ([current platform audit](../research_notes/India%20billing%20platform%20strategy/current_platform_audit.md), [package scripts](../package.json))

## Build the accounting and GST foundations together

The biggest product gap is a **double-entry accounting core** linked to every business transaction. Create a chart of accounts and journal/voucher engine, financial years, opening balances, general ledger, receivable/payable subledgers, cash and bank books, trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, daybook, bank reconciliation, period close/lock, and reversals. An invoice should post sales, tax liability, receivable, and stock/cost effects; a supplier bill should post inventory or expense, input-tax accounts, and payable; receipts/payments should allocate against open items and cash/bank; returns and notes should reverse or adjust the right linked entries. Keep source documents traceable to balanced postings instead of allowing separate screens to create unconnected totals. These are product architecture recommendations, not claims about competitors’ internals.

GST must be a **document and return pipeline**, not a percentage on an item. CGST Rule 46 includes supplier/recipient facts, financial-year-unique serial and date, HSN/SAC, quantity/UQC for goods, taxable value after discounts, tax rates and components, place-of-supply details for inter-State cases, delivery address where relevant, reverse-charge indication, and signature requirements. Composition businesses, exempt supplies, notes, and other document types have different treatment. Place of supply, seller registration, buyer GST status, ship-to/bill-to, document date, transaction purpose, tax classification, cess, and applicable exemptions all affect what the invoice and accounts need to record. ([CBIC invoice rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/gst-invoice-rules.html), [IGST Act](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/IGST-bill-e.html), [composition rules](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/composition-rules.html))

Implement GST in layers. First, represent each GSTIN, legal entity/state, registration type and dates, financial year, invoice series, and return cadence. Add effective-dated HSN/SAC and tax classifications, distinguish taxable/nil/exempt/non-GST/zero-rated supplies, record cess and rate provenance, and retain the exact tax inputs and calculations on each issued document. Then calculate CGST/SGST/UTGST versus IGST from validated registration and supply facts, with review warnings where classification or place-of-supply facts are uncertain. Provide invoice, bill-of-supply, credit/debit-note, cancellation and amendment lifecycles with immutable issued snapshots and reasoned corrections. The 2025 GST rate changes and ongoing notification-driven rate schedules are direct evidence against a fixed small slab list. ([CBIC GST rates](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/gst-goods-services-rates.html), [56th GST Council implementation material](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555&lang=2&reg=3), [compliance research](../research_notes/India%20billing%20platform%20strategy/gst_compliance.md))

Next add reviewable GSTR-1/IFF and GSTR-3B period workspaces, exports aligned to current portal tables, return status and filed snapshots, and purchase-side GSTR-2B/IMS matching. Show Ledgerwise books, portal values, user adjustments, and mismatches together; classify possible missing, duplicate, period, GSTIN, tax, or amendment issues. GSTR-2B supports ITC work but does not decide every legally available credit, so the software should assist review rather than promise automated legal entitlement. Confirm current GSTN filing/API access, authorization, and partner arrangements before committing to direct filing. ([GST Portal GSTR-1 guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Creation_of_Outward_Supplies_Return_in_GSTR_1.htm), [GSTR-3B guide](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/Create_and_Submit_GSTR3B.htm), [GSTR-2B FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/userguide/returns/FAQ_gstr2b.htm), [GSTN IMS FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/news/final_faqs_on_ims_22_09_2024.pdf))

Treat e-invoicing and e-way bills as separate integrations with eligibility checks, schema versions, request/response records, acknowledgements, retries, duplicate protection, error states, cancellations/replacements, and status visible to users. The IRP mandate page states the threshold as AATO ₹5 crore or more in any financial year from FY 2017–18 onward, with exclusions; historical PAN-wide turnover and taxpayer category matter. From 1 April 2025, businesses with AATO ₹10 crore or more must report covered invoices and notes within 30 days. The general e-way bill threshold is ₹50,000, subject to exemptions and state-specific rules. The 2026 portal material records proposed changes later put on hold, showing why current advisories must be checked before a release. October 2026 GST Council recommendations with 2027 targets are recommendations, not current law. ([IRP mandate](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/einvoice-mandate/), [IRP time-limit advisory](https://einvoice6.gst.gov.in/content/revised-time-limit-for-e-invoice-reporting-for-businesses-with-aato-of-%E2%82%B910-crores-above/), [NIC E-Way Bill portal](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/), [NIC API release notes](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/apidocs/release-notes.html), [57th Council recommendations](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2320934&lang=1&reg=3))

## Sequence the roadmap around a paying pilot

| Stage | Build and validate | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Discovery** | Interview and observe 20–30 owners, cashiers, bookkeepers, and CAs in one city and vertical. Map purchase, sale, returns, credit, stock, GST close, legacy records, printer/scanner use, and connectivity. Recruit 5–10 design partners. | Users repeatedly complete the target workflow; pilot businesses agree to use it with real records and discuss a paid plan. |
| **1. Reliable daily operations** | Split the monolithic page into domain modules; source-control schema/migrations and backend functions; add roles, audit history, backups/export, pagination, validation, idempotent writes, financial-year numbering, document lifecycle (returns/notes/cancellations), payment allocation, statements, migration/import, keyboard-first billing and thermal print. | Reconcile opening balances; invoice, purchase, stock, and payment flows remain correct through partial payments, returns, retries, and concurrent use. |
| **2. Accounting and GST readiness** | Implement balanced double-entry postings and financial statements; seller GST registrations; effective-dated tax rules; explainable tax calculation; GSTR-1/3B preparation/export; GSTR-2B/IMS reconciliation; exception/reviewer workflow; accountant access; GST practitioner review. | Pilot books reconcile to source transactions and GST reports reconcile to a GST professional’s sample return cases. No legal compliance claims based only on a successful PDF export. |
| **3. Shop and wholesale depth** | Add purchase/sales orders, delivery challans, hold/resume billing, barcode lookup, multiple units/conversions, price lists and credit limits, stock valuation, reorder, godowns/transfers, and batch/expiry/serial where the chosen vertical needs them. Add IRP and e-way-bill integration after eligibility and access are confirmed. | Measured speed/reliability at real counters; lower stock and reconciliation errors; customers renew and support burden is understood. |
| **4. Scale for larger organizations** | Add multiple GSTINs/branches, approvals, consolidated reporting, fine-grained permissions, bank feeds/reconciliation, APIs/e-commerce connectors, advanced inventory/manufacturing, stronger admin controls, and configurable implementation/support. | A repeatable sales, onboarding, migration, security, and support model exists for multi-location customers. |

This sequencing is an inference from Ledgerwise’s current primitives, competitor workflows, and GST requirements, not a proven forecast. Large-business features such as consolidated entities, approval matrices, complex warehouse costing, manufacturing, and custom integrations should wait until a specific customer segment funds them. Keep service-business functionality—quotes, recurring invoices, projects, time billing, and customer portals—as a separate expansion track rather than distracting the first shop/wholesale pilot.

## Make migration, reliability and support part of the product

A buyer moving from Tally, BUSY, Vyapar, spreadsheets, or paper needs more than CSV upload. Build guided import with field mapping, duplicate detection, row-level errors, opening stock/receivable/payable balances, preview totals, reconciliation, and rollback. Tally documents migration templates and exception handling; myBillBook advertises import paths for competing systems, indicating that switching support is visible in the category. Measure time to first invoice, time to reconciled opening balances, time to first reviewed GST period, 30/90-day retained active businesses, invoice volume, failed operations, and support time per customer. ([Tally import guide](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/data-management/import-data-in-tally/), [myBillBook retail/migration](https://mybillbook.in/s/billing-software-for-retail-shop/))

Architect around tenant → GST registration → branch/location → user/role, with transactional documents, immutable issued snapshots, stock movements, payment allocations, ledger postings, and compliance submissions as separate but linked records. Enforce tenant boundaries and business rules in the database; use unique per-registration/document-type/financial-year numbering, balanced posting constraints, idempotency, audit events, and period locks. Replace full client-side loading and reporting with server-side pagination and aggregation. The README says RLS is enabled, but the checked-in repo does not contain the deployed policy/function definitions, so audit the live backend and bring its source of truth into version control before relying on that claim. This architecture is a recommendation, not an observed description of any competitor system. ([current platform audit](../research_notes/India%20billing%20platform%20strategy/current_platform_audit.md))

Reliability at a counter is a product feature. Test actual keyboards, scanners, thermal printers, UPI/cash/card settlement, and weak-network behavior with pilot shops. If offline billing is needed, build an explicit local queue, idempotent sync, conflict handling, and visible unsynced status rather than claiming offline support from a cached web page. Add backup/restore, monitoring, recovery paths, permission controls, user activity history, and clear customer support escalation. UPI is widespread in the cited merchant survey, but it also reports connectivity and fraud concerns; support cash and manual payment reconciliation too. ([DFS/Ipsos merchant impact study](https://www.financialservices.gov.in/beta/sites/default/files/socio-economic-impact-incentive-scheme.pdf))

Commercially, test simple packaging rather than copying volatile list prices: a single-shop plan for core sales, stock and books; a growth plan for multiple users, locations and advanced inventory; and an accountant/enterprise tier for GST workflow, controls, integrations and assisted onboarding. Test willingness to pay in live pilots and track acquisition cost, support hours, retention, expansion, and gross margin. Established competitor partner networks make training and implementation part of the competitive landscape; a regional CA/bookkeeper channel could help distribution, but needs enablement, permissions, data ownership rules, and economics validation. There is not enough public data to assert an optimal price or a guaranteed unmet niche. ([Zoho Books plans](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/pricing/), [Tally partner network](https://tallysolutions.com/partners/), [BUSY partner network](https://busy.in/our-network/))

## Conclusion

Ledgerwise should aim to be the system that makes one shop’s daily sales, stock, cash, customer dues, supplier bills, accounting entries, and GST review agree with each other. That reliability—combined with practical migration and help at onboarding—is a more testable advantage than matching every competitor’s feature list. Start with a paid, narrow pilot, build the transaction and accounting core beneath the current interface, and widen into multi-GSTIN and enterprise workflows only after the core is trusted in daily use.

The main uncertainty is commercial, not technical: public research cannot prove which Indian retailer/wholesaler segment will switch, what it will pay, or what support load it creates. Resolve that with observed workflows and retained paid pilots. Treat GST law, portal schemas, thresholds, and government API access as maintained operational dependencies, with an accountant/GST practitioner reviewing cases before Ledgerwise claims production compliance.
